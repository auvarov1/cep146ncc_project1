# Subtopic "Accountability and Responsibility"

In this part we will be talking about Accountability and Responsibility when we are using AI for software development. As AI becomes more autonomous and grow in different critical spheres of life traditional quality and control methods stop working.

The first key aspect is Accountability. In academic concepts, it means the relationship between an actor and an external forum, where the person must explain their decisions. By Hohma E., 2003 'Investigating accountability for 
Artificial Intelligence through risk governance' accountability consist of:
- the responsible actors (Developers and Engineering Teams), 
- the forum to whom the account is directed (Tech Leads and Architecture Review Board),
- the relationship of accountability between stakeholders and the forum (Traceability and Lineage), 
- the criteria that are to be fulfilled to reach sufficient account (Acceptance Criteria, KPIs and Compliance Standards),
- the consequences, good or bad, for the accountable parties (Incident Response, Mitigation and Penalties).

However, in reality, we have problems with moral accountability. Developers almost never know how to explain the decision of complex models, and customers and autorities don't have understandable explanation. 

Second important challenge is Responsibility. Here, a so-called culpability gap arises. Our society usualy looks for concrete people to hold accountable. But if a mistake is made by an autonomous algorithm, it makes no sense to punish machine itself, and holding developers is also not easy. And the "black box" add additional problem, because the decision-making logic is hidden even from the creators themselves.

To see how this works in real life, consider the Air Canada chatbot case: an airline’s customer service chatbot mistakenly promised a passenger a special discount. When the passenger tried to claim it, the company refused, arguing that the chatbot's output was incorrect and that it operates autonomously. However, the tribunal ruled against the airline, stating clearly that a company is fully responsible for everything its system communicates—you cannot simply shift the blame onto an AI.

In conclusion, it is impossible to solve these problems without new standards of transparency, rigorous system audits, and reliable state regulation.
